Proposal for a lightweight research tool

Tone and Assurance Testing

A practical way to test sensitive service wording with the people who need it, the teams who must publish it, and the specialists who check plain language and accessibility.

Use it to support content decisions Start with victim information content

The problem

Good intentions are not enough for high sensitivity content.

A message can feel warm to the public but promise more than the organisation can deliver. Another message can be legally accurate but feel cold, blaming, or unclear to victims. Teams need a safe way to compare wording before publication.

Recommended decision rule

Rank by evidence. Publish only if risk gates pass.

What it is

A three stream validation method for wording decisions

Tone and Assurance Testing is a lightweight research method that combines public response, organisational assurance, and technical content quality checks. It does not replace formal approval. It gives teams better evidence before approval.

Audience evidence

Tests whether people understand the message, trust it, feel respected, and know what to do next.

Comms and policy assurance

Checks whether the message is accurate, publishable, aligned to policy, and does not promise more than the organisation can deliver.

Plain language and accessibility review

Checks whether the wording is clear, structured, readable, and suitable for people using different devices, assistive technologies, and literacy levels.

How it works

A simple workflow that fits into content writing

The tool is designed to support research and writing during a live project. It is intentionally lighter than a full approval system, but it still records evidence, risk checks, and decisions.

1

Set the scenario

Define the user situation, content goal, target audience, sensitivity level, and the decision that the test needs to support.

2

Prepare wording options

Add two to four versions of the message. Each version should be realistic and safe enough to test with participants.

3

Run three review streams

Audience participants test understanding and response. Comms and policy reviewers test publishability. Plain language and accessibility reviewers test clarity and inclusion.

4

Apply risk gates

The tool checks critical gates such as policy accuracy, operational promise, safety, harm and blame, privacy, and accessibility.

5

Generate a recommendation

The report ranks versions by evidence strength, shows risks, lists required edits, and records the decision for future audit and learning.

Risk gates protect the decision

A high score should not automatically select a version. If a message fails a critical gate, it should be marked as not publishable until it is revised and reviewed again.

  • Policy accuracy
  • Operational promise
  • Safety risk
  • Harm, blame, and stigma
  • Privacy and consent
  • Accessibility and readability

Suggested scoring model

The score is configurable. For victim information content, the model should give strong weight to audience evidence, while still protecting institutional accountability and accessibility.

Audience Evidence Score
35%
Clear understanding, correct next step, trust, respect, support, and confidence to act.
Comms and Policy Assurance Score
30%
Policy fit, operational accuracy, legal risk, publishability, and tone of voice fit.
Plain Language and Accessibility Score
20%
Plain language, structure, readability, cognitive load, WCAG related content checks, and inclusive wording.
Evidence Confidence Score
15%
Sample relevance, number of responses, segment coverage, reviewer quality, and strength of supporting comments.

Why it matters

Better wording decisions for sensitive services

Victim information content must be clear, respectful, safe, and operationally honest. This tool helps teams test these qualities before publication, not after harm or confusion has already occurred.

Reduces the risk of publishing content that sounds helpful but creates unsafe or unrealistic expectations.

Gives content designers evidence before final wording decisions are made.

Helps comms, policy, operations, accessibility, and research teams discuss wording with a shared framework.

Creates a decision record that explains why a version was chosen, what risks were checked, and what still needs review.

Supports sensitive service content, especially victim information, where tone, trust, safety, and operational accuracy all matter.

What makes it different from existing tools

Existing UX platforms can run surveys, preference tests, tree tests, card sorts, first click tests, and prototype tests. This proposal adds a government focused decision layer that combines public understanding, institutional assurance, accessibility, risk gates, and a reusable decision record.

Recommended first pilot

Run one pilot on a high sensitivity victim information message. Test three wording options with a small audience group, two comms or policy reviewers, and one plain language or accessibility reviewer. Use the report to decide whether the method gives enough value to add to the existing tree test platform.

Pilot success measure

The team can clearly explain which version is safer, clearer, more publishable, and why.
