Tone Test

A lightweight way to turn sensitive wording decisions into shared evidence, especially when advisors need agency feedback on tone, policy fit, user understanding, plain language, and accessibility.

Start with the pain point See the value

Style helps people write. Tone Test helps teams decide.

A style guide can guide wording, but it cannot confirm whether a specific piece of content works for the audience, the agency, the policy position, and digital content requirements.

The value is evidence before wording decisions become delays.

Pain point

Cross-agency writing often depends on repeated review cycles

In cross-agency projects, advisors often write content when they are not the policy or publishing owner. They may have a style guide, or they may not. Either way, the final wording still needs judgement from the right people.

What happens now

Teams write multiple versions, send them around, wait for comments, discuss feedback in meetings, and revise again. The feedback is useful, but it is often hard to compare, hard to trace, and hard to reuse.

Why it matters

Sensitive wording is more than style

For victim information and other high sensitivity content, wording affects trust, safety, policy accuracy, service expectations, plain language, and accessibility. A version can sound supportive but overpromise. Another can be accurate but unclear or hard to act on.

1

Audience evidence

Checks whether people understand the message, trust it, feel respected, and know what to do next.

2

Agency assurance

Checks tone, policy fit, operational boundaries, and whether the wording overpromises.

3

Content quality

Checks plain language, readability, structure, accessibility needs, and digital content quality.

What Tone Test does

One online place for asynchronous feedback

Participants do not need accounts. They open one link, choose their role, and answer questions matched to their responsibility. This supports digital ways of working without turning the tool into a formal approval system.

1

Create

Add the scenario, content goal, and two to four wording variants.

2

Share

Send one public link to audience participants, agency reviewers, and content specialists.

3

Collect

Capture scores, comments, preferences, and risk gate responses by role.

4

Use

Review a simple evidence summary and export CSV if deeper analysis is needed.

Meaning and value

From wording debate to evidence-based content decisions

  • ✓
    Reduces repeated first-round feedback loops.
  • ✓
    Separates audience, agency, and accessibility perspectives.
  • ✓
    Creates a reusable evidence base for future wording decisions.
  • ✓
    Uses risk gates so high scores do not hide policy, safety, privacy, or accessibility concerns.

What the team gets

A clearer explanation of which wording option is easier to understand, safer to use, more publishable, and why. The tool supports human judgement instead of replacing it.

Expected outcome

A reusable tool for government content work

Tone Test aligns with evidence-based government work and digital workflow improvement. It helps teams capture structured feedback online, reduce reliance on synchronous meetings, and make content decisions easier to explain.

Core value

The team can say which version is clearer, safer, more publishable, and why.

Tone Test supports research and content decisions. It does not replace policy, legal, operational, privacy, comms, or accessibility approval.
